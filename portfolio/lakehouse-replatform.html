The challenge

BlueSprig reports monthly to its private equity sponsor and runs clinical operations across more than 150 sites. Its reporting sat on a legacy SQL Server and Power BI estate that could not keep pace with either.

Three problems compounded. Routine operational reporting took weeks of manual work. The estate cost $40K a month across SQL Server licensing, Azure Data Factory runs, Power BI licensing and Microsoft Fabric. And every new question from Finance or Clinical Operations waited in a queue behind an analyst.

For a sponsor-backed provider, weeks of reporting latency is a business problem, not a technology one: decisions were being made on numbers that were already out of date.

The approach

The conventional answer was a migration to a large cloud data warehouse. We chose a different path, built on three decisions.

  1. Size the platform to the workload. We rebuilt the estate as a DuckLake lakehouse on MotherDuck (DuckDB), whose serverless pricing matched the workload BlueSprig actually has: 300+ data models drawn from eight source systems. A warehouse sized for a far larger enterprise would have carried the old cost structure forward.
  2. Run the data platform as software. Hand-run refresh jobs gave way to scheduled, observable pipelines in Prefect Cloud. Soda validates the data continuously, so quality failures surface before stakeholders see them. Every change is version-controlled in GitHub and released through CI/CD.
  3. Migrate with the business. Finance and Clinical Operations were treated as the customers of a product launch, so adoption was planned from the first day. The estate moved without a single big-bang cutover.

The impact

  • Cost. Data platform spend fell from $40K to under $10K a month, a saving of more than $360K a year.
  • Delivery. A team of three moved 300+ data models from eight source systems in ten months.
  • Speed. Reporting that took weeks of manual work became self-service.
  • Trust. The board package now draws from governed, continuously validated data.
  • Foundation. The platform underpins an AI-assisted analytics layer that lets non-technical stakeholders query the lakehouse directly.

What made the difference

Two choices mattered more than any single technology. The first was to select proven components sized to the real workload, and to set aside vendor reference architectures designed for larger organizations. The second was to manage the migration as a change program for Finance and Clinical Operations, with adoption as a goal from the outset.
